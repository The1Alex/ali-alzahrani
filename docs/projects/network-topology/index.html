Mapping a whole plant's network

Recovered and documented the factory network from the machines on the floor up to core group switching, so incidents could be diagnosed from a desk.

My role
Led
Status
Completed two-year programme
Position
Infrastructure Expert & Project Manager

1The challenge

Every network incident meant walking the plant to trace connections by hand before the real investigation could even begin. No complete picture of the network existed, from the automation layer up to the core.

2My responsibility

I decided to create the full picture and led the programme. I organised the site discovery and produced the documentation. The central network team configured the monitoring tool, and I gave them accurate site data for it.

3My reasoning and decisions

  • Use a planned December shutdown for the site survey, to avoid disturbing production.
  • Bring in one contractor and walk the plant systematically, identifying switches, ports and connections, over about six days.
  • Draw the diagrams in a simple tool everyone could open and update: PowerPoint.
  • Feed the validated data to the network team so that it could become live monitoring maps in Zabbix.

4The solution

Over a two-year programme delivered alongside routine operations, I recovered and defined the complete topology from the automation layer (IT/OT) through to core group switching, standardised interlinked site connectivity, and contributed architecture plans and recommendations. This work also supported the re-architecture of more than 100 Cisco and N-Tron switches to reduce latency.

5The result

When a station later lost its network, the team used the diagrams and remote checks to identify the affected switch and go straight to it, instead of searching switch by switch. We can now also tell much faster whether a problem lies in the network, the PC or the machine.

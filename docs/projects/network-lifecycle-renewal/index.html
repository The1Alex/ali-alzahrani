Renewing ageing switches without disruption

Spotted switches approaching the end of support in my inventory, made the risk case for budget, then replaced them building by building during shutdowns.

My role
Led
Status
Completed; a routine repeated every two to three years
Position
Middle East IT Leader

1The challenge

The inventory I keep showed a group of switches about to become outdated, which would mean no support, no software updates and a direct security risk.

2My responsibility

I raised the request, justified the budget, ordered the equipment, and planned and carried out the replacement with my team.

3My reasoning and decisions

  • Make the budget case in terms of risk: no support, no updates, a security exposure.
  • Rehearse first. We built a replica of each live switch's configuration in the IT office and tested it.
  • Replace in stages, building by building and during shutdowns, with the new switch above the old one, moving ports one by one.
  • Verify at the application level, not just with a ping: test one device per VLAN and check that attendance machines appeared in their system.

4The solution

Each switch was named, configured and tested before deployment, then confirmed online in Zabbix after replacement. We told each building about the change, updated the records with the new dates and versions, and arranged recycling through Sourcing.

5The result

The replacements checked out and users reported no issues afterwards. No formal disruption metric was recorded.
